🔑 What an EPP code is

A domain can’t move between registrars without its EPP Auth Info — sometimes called a “domain secret,” “transfer key,” or “domain password.” It exists to protect the domain’s owner: nobody can transfer a domain away without proving they hold it. Every registrar is obliged to provide it on request; SiteGround kept it until asked.

📬 Three alarms, one missing code

The domain’s hosting move had gone through in January with A records re-pointed — but the registrar-level transfer itself sat pending, waiting on that code. WPX’s migration experts sent the same polite alarm three times over five weeks:

  1. #749349 — Kristian S., March 10
  2. #528865 — Tsvetomir P., April 2
  3. #743286 — Monika D., April 15

Each explained exactly what was needed and how to obtain it. Once the EPP Auth Info was provided, the transfer went through — and by the time of the May server-health morning, all four websites were fully on WPX, HTTPS forced, the XDN in front of everything.

🧠 The lesson in the subject line

“IMPORTANT! Domain transfer failed” is a frightening subject line for what was really a missing form field. A pattern this desk keeps meeting: the scariest alarm is often the smallest key. When an automated system says “failed,” read what it’s actually asking for before assuming the building is on fire.

The sign on the door, the deed, and the keys — all in the same drawer now.

Shared in Discord, WhatsApp or iMessage, it arrives with its own preview card and its own star.

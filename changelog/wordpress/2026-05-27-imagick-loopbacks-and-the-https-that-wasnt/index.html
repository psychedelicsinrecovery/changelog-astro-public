Imagick, loopbacks, and the https that wasn't

Medium 2026-05-27WordPress sites 2 min readBy Christopher W
TL;DR: A live-chat morning with WPX that settled a whole season of Site Health warnings in one sitting: imagick installed on all four sites, the firewall taught that our own server IPs are friends (the whitelisting that later made agent automation safe), MariaDB 10.5 and no Redis declared features rather than flaws - and the service site's https error turning out to be one missing "s" in our own URL settings.

Source: WPX live chat #TF4OYJ9P9Z, logged as ticket #388799 (WPX support portal).

Read the full story →

The asks, and where they landed

Christopher came to the chat with three requests and stayed for a tour of the whole basement:

  1. Imagick - installed first for the service site (Martin G.), then, when Christopher asked the right follow-up question, extended to all four websites: main, service, newsletter, publications (Denis S.).
  2. The loopback 400s - Site Health’s complaint that the server couldn’t talk to itself. WPX’s logs showed the real story: their firewall had been denying REST API access to an ASN it flagged as “mimicking Chrome.” The fix: a permanent whitelist of the server’s own IPs (194.1.147.16 and 194.1.147.34) and standard user-agent strings, so internal REST loopbacks - and the automation tooling the Tech Committee was openly planning - would never be intercepted as a “malicious hacker script.”
  3. MariaDB 10.6+ - declined, on purpose: shared host, the version is a deliberate stability choice, and WordPress 7.0 installs happily on 10.3+. The badge is cosmetic.

The rest of the tour

  • Redis object cache: not available on the WordPress plan (WooCommerce/Agency only), and “not required either” at our scale.
  • PHP 8.4 held (8.5 available; Elementor/Astra caution won the day).
  • The https error on the subdomain - Christopher solved it himself mid-chat: the WordPress and Site Address URLs in Settings → General were missing the “s.” Manually corrected, saved, error gone.
  • The zip module ghost - server-side present and working, yet Site Health kept insisting it was missing. PHP version swap didn’t clear it; cache-flush rituals didn’t clear it. Filed under known-benign, never to be chased again.
  • UpdraftPlus restored by adding RewriteRule .* - [E=noabort:1] to the .htaccess files.

Why this morning matters

The whitelist is the quiet headliner. It’s the reason background tasks, cron-driven updates, and later the Tech Committee’s API-driven automation could talk to the sites without tripping the firewall. The chat also produced the fellowship’s working list of ignorable warnings - so no future volunteer burns an evening on the MariaDB badge or the zip ghost. (Gemini’s suggestion to add WP_DISABLE_DEVELOPMENT_PLUGINS to wp-config was politely declined - the constant isn’t real for this purpose, and Denis S. said as much.)

Sometimes the fix is a letter. Sometimes it’s a whitelist.

Shared in Discord, WhatsApp or iMessage, it arrives with its own preview card and its own star.

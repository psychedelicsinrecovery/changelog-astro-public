Service is something we hold together

High 2026-11-21GSR Committee 5 min readBy GSR Subcommittee
TL;DR: November's GSR meeting put a name to something the fellowship has been feeling: readiness for service is personal reflection and shared responsibility, held together. Rick B brought a proposed Recovery Readiness for Service document - a draft, not a verdict - and GSRs took three questions home to their groups. Karyn S is stepping down as GSR Chair. And somewhere in Ann Arbor, a hybrid meeting needs a Zoom link. This story accompanies, rather than replaces, the chair's complete record, which mirrors the minutes in full and transcribes the proposed draft.
Unfold the story of the morning

Filed for Psychedelics In Recovery™ – PIR® from the November 21, 2026 GSR Subcommittee meeting and the chair’s final minutes. Names are shared as they appear in the minutes; personal recovery disclosures from the recorded discussion are intentionally not reproduced here.

A draft, not a verdict

The full proposed text is transcribed, clearly labeled as not adopted, in the full record. The draft asks about six ordinary, difficult things: Am I presently able and willing to fulfill the responsibilities of this position reliably? Can I acknowledge mistakes, accept feedback, and take responsibility for my actions? Am I exercising sound judgment, maintaining appropriate boundaries, and respecting the trust placed in me? Are there circumstances, behaviors, or challenges in my life that may interfere with my ability to serve responsibly? Am I familiar with PIR’s Twelve Traditions and willing to practice their principles? Am I open to guidance, support, and, when appropriate, stepping back temporarily to care for myself and others?

The answers came from every direction of the room. One member suggested that a period free of substances might qualify someone for service; another answered that PIR includes people from fellowships where substances never factor in at all, and that even where they do, no timeline is fair. One member said the document helped her see it is okay to ask for help - that missing a GSR meeting while being a single mom who works in recovery is not failing her community. Another asked the room to remember that service is giving back when ready, not the therapy itself, and that the fellowship does not owe anyone a position they may confuse with their recovery.

Rick B, who brought the draft, said it was originally written more stridently and had been reworked toward self-reflection after conversations with Douglas. The opening pages now read like a mirror rather than a checklist.

Three questions to take home

Karyn S charged every GSR with taking the draft back to their home group and returning in December with answers to three questions:

  1. Does your group want to apply this document to your group?
  2. Does your group want to see this document applied to the GSR Subcommittee?
  3. Should it be posted on the PIR website with the other member materials?

A group does not have to return with only a yes or a no. It may come back with a recommendation to amend the document, and the subcommittee would take the amended version through the process again. This was discussion, not a vote; adoption and publication approval were not given at GSR.

The chair’s chair

Karyn S announced she is stepping down as GSR Chair. Nominations and outreach are underway - a couple of people are being approached about readiness and interest, and the idea of a nomination form, floated in discussion, echoes how the board’s first nominations were gathered. Rick B credited Karyn S for leading the room with patience through a heavy agenda.

Parking lots, and where the meeting ends

The Wednesday UK/Europe meeting reported that its parking lot - the unstructured conversation space around the online meeting - has been hosting monologues that leave members uncomfortable, and asked how other groups have handled it lovingly, and where a meeting’s responsibility ends. The discussion surfaced the existing Safety Guidelines, money-exchange history, and the idea, raised by Erin B in chat, of a Parking Lot Monitor service commitment: the parking lot held to the same safety levels as the meeting, with no bullying or dominating. No solution was settled; the question stays open for the groups. A plain-language explainer of the existing guidance is being drafted for committee review - a draft only, not new policy.

Hybrid, and the cost of showing up twice

Salt Lake City reported that few people join online, that setting up the hybrid portion is a hassle, and that the group is considering in-person only - while not wanting to take the opportunity away from anyone attending online. Karyn S suggested finding an open calendar slot; Anne R’s experience across fellowships was that hybrid formats tend to split rooms rather than join them, and that multiple attempts in PIR have struggled. The group may test another month with an updated listing. The website’s Salt Lake City listing was corrected on November 18 to 7 pm Mountain; Shaynna G has been in touch with Christopher about anything further.

Ann Arbor’s in-person group, by contrast, wants hybrid to work: Jodi H needs a Zoom link set up for its hybrid meeting, and Christopher and Jodi H will connect after the meeting. The TechCom desk has more on that room’s side of the work.

Small rooms, shared load

In the written reports, open positions dot the fellowship’s map: chairs and tech hosts, GSRs, greeters, a new leader for the Wednesday Step Study. Christy B continues as Traveling GSR and will cover Monday Entheon until a GSR is selected. Jaime I reopened the co-traveler and sponsorship list - fewer than 20 people - and invited anyone who feels the call to sponsor or co-travel to reach out. And the discussion kept returning to one theme: service is a shared responsibility, and a group that appoints a trusted servant holds them, checks in with them, and does not hand them the whole website alone.

Recovery Readiness for Service A Proposed Statement for Service in Psychedelics in Recovery (PIR)

Psychedelics in Recovery recognizes each person’s freedom to define their own recovery. As a 12 Step Fellowship, we embrace diverse pathways and honor personal autonomy without suggesting a single definition of sobriety or recovery.

Service is an opportunity to give back, practice spiritual principles, and contribute to the well-being of our fellowship. It is also a responsibility that invites honest self-reflection about our present capacity to serve.

Accountability in service is a form of care, for ourselves, for those we serve, and for the fellowship as a whole. Readiness is not about perfection, diagnosis, or judging the validity of anyone’s recovery. It is about recognizing our strengths, limitations, and ability to fulfill the responsibilities entrusted to us.

A self-reflection guide. Members considering or currently holding service positions are invited to reflect on the following questions:

  • Commitment. Am I presently able and willing to fulfill the responsibilities of this position reliably?
  • Accountability. Can I acknowledge mistakes, accept feedback, and take responsibility for my actions?
  • Judgment and boundaries. Am I exercising sound judgment, maintaining appropriate boundaries, and respecting the trust placed in me?
  • Personal readiness. Are there circumstances, behaviors, or challenges in my life that may interfere with my ability to serve responsibly?
  • Fellowship principles. Am I familiar with PIR’s Twelve Traditions and willing to practice their principles, including humility, unity, loving-kindness, and principles before personalities?
  • Willingness to grow. Am I open to guidance, support, and, when appropriate, stepping back temporarily to care for myself and others?

Loving group conscience. While self-reflection is the foundation of service readiness, service is also a shared responsibility. When concerns arise that materially affect a member’s ability to fulfill their role, or affect PIR as a whole, loving group conscience and the appropriate service body may help guide a compassionate, fair, and respectful response, consistent with PIR’s Traditions, bylaws, and established procedures.

Recovery is a personal journey. Service is a collective responsibility.

Discussion

  • Anne R: She thinks it is very good and particularly likes the closing line, which she described as where the steps meet the traditions. She said her recovery is completely correlated to her fellowship and she needs both. Service does not come naturally in PIR for some reason and it is about time that culture is brought in. She cited AA statistics suggesting about 14 percent of people do service, said PIR is lower than that, and said people will do service only if they understand how beneficial it is to them and to the fellowship. She called it a very welcome addition to PIR literature.
  • Evan K: He concurred and called it sensitively written. He suggested adding some way of dealing with an understanding of how a member’s actions in PIR affect others. He said one of the reasons the document is being discussed is an incident that really ruptured the organization, and that he does not hear people talking about the rupture itself, which affects his sense of safety in his recovery and, much more directly, other people’s sense of physical safety in recovery. Since unity is one of PIR’s important aspects, understanding how one’s actions impact others seems a wise part of the document and the discussion.
  • Alan H: He said the document captures the sentiment and tone of PIR. While it notes that PIR does not define sobriety for members because it is self-defined, it might be helpful to suggest a certain length of time free of substances as a recommendation for qualification for service, as in AA where a year of sobriety might be required for treasurer. He said that could also be left up to the groups. He noted that servants at group level are voted in by group conscience, so the group hopefully has a sense of an individual’s readiness. He added that the strength of sponsorship is lacking in PIR compared with conventional AA, and that for him sponsorship was a huge factor in readiness for service, since he would discuss a service position with his sponsor first.
  • Rick B: He had a strong reaction to Alan H’s suggestion of a length of time free of substances, while recognizing it as well intended. PIR includes too many people from different 12-step fellowships where substances do not factor in, and he cannot conceive of a responsible way to do it. Even where substances are involved, the timeline is too hard to pin down.
  • Shaynna G: She has missed a lot of GSR meetings. She has been chair, co-chair and GSR for the Salt Lake City meeting, which is a small group of five or six people who show up consistently and who step in for each other and rotate positions. She works in recovery and is a single mom, and cannot help missing meetings sometimes. Showing up and staying true to her responsibilities has been important in her recovery. She said people coming into recovery sometimes take responsibility so seriously that they hinder their own mental health or recovery, and that the document helped her recognize it is okay to ask for help and okay to miss a GSR meeting so long as she brings things back to her group.
  • Jaime I: Following Alan H’s point, he raised the co-traveler and sponsorship document he has been managing with Karyn S. The list is small, fewer than 20 people. He asked anyone who feels the call to sponsor or co-travel to get in touch so they can be added, and suggested GSRs take the list back to their home groups. He posted his phone number in the chat.
  • Sue: She noted that not everyone comes from AA, that service can be about integrating psychedelics and what members are learning, and that the messaging can differ from AA. She suggested baby steps, since running the timer at a meeting is a start where joining the tech committee may be overwhelming, and said perfectionism stops people from starting at all.
  • Evan K: He said that in AA the therapy is understood to be the steps, the meetings and the fellowship, rather than service, and that he carries an assumption that service is giving back when he is ready to do so, with the benefit to his recovery being more indirect. He cautioned against assuming the fellowship owes everyone the right to sit in a service position, because a member may conflate ego and program, and the loss of a position, with their recovery. He said it is complex, which is why it is called a loving group conscience, and named the tension between the individual and unity and the health of the organization as a whole.
  • Rick B: He said the draft was originally written more stridently and was modified considerably after discussion with Douglas, to refocus it on self-reflection and self-accountability while retaining the loving group conscience element.

Scope of the document

  • Karyn S: She asked whether the document is for GSRs to take back to their groups to vote on, and set out that a group could vote to apply it to that group, the GSR Subcommittee could vote to apply it to the subcommittee, and the board could vote to apply it to the board.
  • Rick B: He said he does not have a conviction either way, and that the question gets to the heart of PIR as a whole versus the autonomy of each group. His initial reaction matches Karyn S’s, that the board can decide if it wants to apply it to itself, each group can decide for itself and each subcommittee can decide for itself. He also saw no reason it could not be voted onto the list of approved literature. He stressed it is not intended to be an enforceable document, but a document most people seem to agree has merit, so that if something comes up a group that has adopted it can point to it. Each domain of service needs to decide whether it is useful to them.
  • Karyn S: She added that the subcommittee could also vote on adding it to the website and member materials, and said her feeling is that it may come back with amendments before it is posted, which could make it a multi-month process.

Charge to GSRs, as given by Karyn S

  • Karyn S instructed every GSR to take the Recovery Readiness document back to their home group and discuss it at their next business meeting, and set out three questions for the group to answer:
  1. Does your group want to apply this document to your group?
  2. Does your group want to see this document applied to the GSR Subcommittee, meaning should the GSR Subcommittee follow it?
  3. Does your group think this document should be posted on the PIR website with the other member materials or resources?
  • Karyn S was explicit that a group does not have to come back with only a yes or a no. A group may instead come back with a recommendation to amend the document, and the subcommittee would then go through the process again on the amended version.
  • Karyn S said these three items will be discussed or voted on at the December meeting, and asked GSRs to contact her with any questions before their business meetings so she can walk them through what is being voted on.

Salt Lake City, hybrid format

Her advice is to stick with the in-person meeting, cherish it and grow it, and leave the online meeting for the online calendar.
